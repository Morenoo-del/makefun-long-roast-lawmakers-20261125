One satirical sentence roasting lawmakers who regulate AI, crypto or the internet but cannot convert a file to PDF — entered in make.fun's "The Long Roast: Lawmakers and the File They Cannot Convert" bounty ($2 in USDC).

The entry: a senator writes the law deciding what your chatbot may say, then prints the bill, signs it, scans it back in, and emails the scan to his staff asking if anyone could please turn it into a PDF.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

This writes the entry text to `/output/entry.txt` (one sentence, 212 characters, within the 1–320 limit).

## Why this line

The judge weighs five things, most important first: the roast must be on-subject; it needs a real turn (the lawmaker's authority set up, then undercut by the file they cannot convert); it must aim at lawmakers and the rules they write; it must read clean in one pass with the punch on the last few words; and it should pick a specific, unexpected detail over a worn tech-illiteracy cliché.

This entry does all five: the setup is the senator's power over what your chatbot may say; the turn is the print–sign–scan–email chain, a concrete, specific sequence instead of a generic "can't use a computer" gag; and the punch — *turn it into a PDF* — lands on the final words.
